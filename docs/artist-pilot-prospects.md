## Selecting a useful first cohort

Choose availability and workflow coverage before name recognition. The owner can
propose one photographer, one physical painter/printmaker, one artist who manages
a website-builder portfolio, and two artists familiar with Nostr. These roles
can overlap, but the final five must meet the familiarity and hosting coverage
requirements in the runbook.

If this public shortlist does not yield three non-Nostr artists, ask an approved
local arts group or independent curator to forward the invitation to volunteers.
Do not scrape member lists. A facilitator should not collect financial history,
demographics, private collector lists or identity documents to screen the cohort.

For each proposed invitee, privately record: source checked, relevant workflow,
uncertainties to ask about, professional route, TJ's approval, invitation date,
response status, and whether further contact was requested. Keep unsuccessful
recruitment visible in the final aggregate; do not list only cooperative artists.

## What the evidence does and does not show

The sources show existing artist workflows, related tools and some historical
requests for help. They do not prove demand for Relay Gate, a price point, referral
commissions, a multiplayer auction, or a Nostr account. The pilot should test
those assumptions through specific tasks and choices. A working alternative may
be the right answer for an artist; record that result rather than steering them
toward a new tool.
