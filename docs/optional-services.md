# Optional services: interview and delivery plan

Working material for studio-tracker #193, checked 2026-10-20. **These are
hypothetical service designs and interview anchors, not published offers.** No
customer has been contacted, recruited, charged or promised delivery through
this work. Prices are USD and are not competitor benchmarks or demand findings.

Start by testing one-time setup and curation. An artist buys a completed task
and a useful handoff, while the software remains usable without a service
account. Hosting should wait until export/restore works and support time is
measured. Referrals are a separate experiment, not the operating budget.

## Rights and boundaries

The repository's [MIT license](../LICENSE) covers its software. It does not
license an artist's paintings, photographs, voice, story, likeness or logo.
Obtain the appropriate permissions for those assets separately. A paid service
does not revoke software rights or impose a fee on independent self-hosting.
The [Open Source Initiative FAQ](https://opensource.org/faq) distinguishes
commercial use from proprietary restrictions and discusses branding separately.

Do not charge for an API call, model call, view or gate traversal. Do not require
a wallet, Nostr account, token or NFT purchase to visit. Do not sell visitors'
identity, browsing history or contact details. An artist's sale receipts are
artist funds, not Relay Gate service revenue. Approved gate referrals on sales
remain **0%**; the voluntary zap design remains in #194.

An artist can choose another installer, curator or host and retain their files.
Paid curation must disclose the commissioning party and any paid inclusion.
Sponsorship must not quietly determine discovery ranking or artistic judgment.

## Three concrete package cards for interviews

All scope and capacity figures below are proposed limits to test. No hosting
vendor cost, supported site-builder list or response capability is established
by these cards. Requote a changed scope as a fixed project; never introduce
automatic per-call, per-view or per-gate charges.

| Card | Proposed result and included scope | Interview anchor | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Own-site setup | One artist, one exhibition, up to 20 approved works and one supplied image per work; one existing supported host; metadata review, order, accessible catalog, portable archive, one correction round and a 45-minute handoff. No image restoration or custom engine development. | $600 fixed; compare the same scope at $300 and $900 only after an unprompted budget question. | Artist approves titles/units/rights; own-site install and clean restore both pass; artist can replace one work without the installer. |
| Curated exhibition | Up to five artists and 20 works; a written theme, selection and sequence, up to 500 words of exhibition text, rights/credit list, two collective review rounds and one curator-led rehearsal. Uses an accepted gallery build; custom 3D production and payment handling excluded. | $1,500 fixed; compare $750 and $2,500 after needs/budget discussion. | Each artist approves their inclusion and text; curator signs off sequence; portable show, accessible view and host handoff pass. |
| Annual care | One supported installation; proposed allowance of 1 GB media storage, 25 GB monthly transfer and two hours of routine support/year; weekly backups and a monthly restore check. Proposed response target: two business days, with a named absence backup. No unlimited revisions, 24/7 monitoring, uptime guarantee or automatic renewal. | $240/year; compare $120 and $360. **Do not offer yet.** | Actual provider costs, backup/restore logs, support hours, exit rehearsal and an available backup operator demonstrate the promised scope. |

The storage/transfer allowances limit managed resources, not public access or
ownership of exports. Give advance notice before a limit is reached, review a
fixed capacity upgrade or migration with the artist, and keep downloads
available. A provider's emergency suspension rules must be disclosed before a
real agreement; this document does not claim control over a future provider.

For setup, review five sample works and the current website before quoting.
State whether gallery pages can live under the artist's domain or require a
subdomain. If the host lacks a supported installation path, record that as a
blocked package rather than promising a workaround. Source-site rights and
robots compliance do not prove artwork copyright permission.

For curation, use a single approval owner for scheduling, while preserving each
artist's veto over their own image, credit and story. A paid exhibition cannot
be represented as an independent award or unsolicited endorsement. An auction
is a separate service and depends on #184; no auction settlement is included.

## Pre-sale gates and proposed service terms

The current default branch is an importer prototype. Separate PRs address
setup (#182), narration (#183), certificates (#185), Nostr integration (#186)
and portable export/restore (#196). An open PR is not a completed integration.
Before any real offer, name a tested, merged build and the supported engine and
host, demonstrate the chosen package end to end, and agree:

1. Exact works, images, rights, units, host and exclusions; a ready-materials
   date and mutually accepted delivery window. A missing engine or permission
   blocks the job before taking payment.
2. A fixed price, applicable taxes determined for the actual service, payment
   timing and acceptance milestones. Interview anchors are not invoices.
3. The included correction rounds and a written change request for extra work.
   Correct delivery defects separately from a change in artistic direction.
4. Cancellation/refund calculation: proposed approach is to document accepted
   completed milestones and return the unearned balance, with any nonrefundable
   third-party cost disclosed and agreed in advance. Final wording and any
   mandatory consumer rights must be checked for the actual jurisdiction.
5. Response hours, absence cover and a clear escalation route. A response target
   is not a promise that every defect can be fixed within that time.
6. Handoff: gallery data, authorized media, stories, rights/credits, public event
   archives if used, install/restore steps and ownership of the hosting account.
   The artist retains their domain and signer; never collect a private key.
7. Exit: no fee to retrieve an ordinary export; proposed hosted export window
   of 30 days after the term, with reminders and a deletion date agreed before
   signup. Public Nostr copies cannot be recalled by deleting a hosted copy.

Certificate preparation can become a fixed service after #185's printer and
signed-master-event checks. Charge for record preparation, calibration and
printing, never for a claim of independent authenticity appraisal. Workshops,
institutional discovery, event production, sponsorship and a disclosed service
directory remain later options already described in #193. Prioritize them only
when a named buyer's problem justifies the added work; avoid building every
model at once.

## Five interviews before a paid test

Use #189's permission and recruitment approach. No interviews are recorded or
sent by this PR. Select three independent artists, one artist-run venue/curator
and one cooperative or educator if willing participants become available. This
small convenience sample is directional evidence, not market-size research.

Ask about their most recent exhibition/site update: who did it, time taken,
what failed, and what they actually paid. Ask what they would keep doing
themselves, which information they are willing to supply, and who approves
spending. Show the exact scope card only after that discussion. Ask the budget
question before presenting the price anchors; record price order so anchoring
is visible. Do not report a polite “interesting” as purchase intent.

For each card record one of: problem absent; interested without action; agreed
sample-material review; requested a written scoped quote; accepted a later
paid pilot after readiness approval. Capture objections and alternatives,
including doing nothing. No deposits or payment links during these interviews.

Proceed to at most five individually authorized pilot jobs only if at least
three interviewees describe a concrete recent problem, two request a scoped
next step, and at least one package can meet its acceptance test at positive
contribution after realistic acquisition costs. These are proposed decision
rules, not achieved results. Re-scope or stop if the problem is weak, the
supported-host path fails, or estimated time consistently exceeds the package.

## Accounting handoff

Keep service revenue separate from artwork sale proceeds, voluntary tips,
artist-funded commissions and taxes collected for others. Record invoice and
service ID, a private customer reference, currency, gross amount, processor and
network fees, settlement reference/time, refunds and net. For Bitcoin, retain
the actual sats and the selected FX source/time separately; this USD calculator
does not convert sats or establish bookkeeping treatment.

The [BTCPay integration guide](https://docs.btcpayserver.org/Development/ecommerce-integration-guide/)
distinguishes payment detection from settled invoices and documents webhook
verification and reconciliation. A visitor's “paid” message or arbitrary zap
receipt is not enough. Test partial, late, overpaid, expired and refunded states
in the separately reviewed private ledger before selling. Export columns and
account mappings need the user's bookkeeper's approval; this PR is not a
QuickBooks importer and does not implement payment collection.

Open decisions: first supported host; service provider's available weekly time
and hourly cost; initial package/scope; response backup; actual printer profile;
bookkeeper-approved service/artist-fund classifications. Replies belong on the
PR. No outreach, sale, deployment or announcement is authorized by this plan.
